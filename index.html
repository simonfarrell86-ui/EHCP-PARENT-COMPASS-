My Story — Why I Built This For You

If you are reading this while exhausted, overwhelmed, or wondering whether anyone is truly listening to you—please take a breath and know you are not alone. I have four children living in the Cheshire East area and schooled/schooling in the area, all four of whom have EHCPs, and I understand firsthand the enormous stress this situation and process can weigh on both parents and children. That is why I created this app—to help families in every way possible.

I truly understand how daunting this whole situation feels when you are trying to hold family life together while learning a system full of jargon, deadlines, and paperwork. Unfortunately, along this journey, you will more than likely come across some very unhelpful, dismissive, and insensitive "professionals." You may be made to feel like "it's all your fault," told "it's not that bad," or handed lines like "funding is tight"—basically any excuse not to put the right support in place.

Hold Onto This Truth

NEVER allow ANYONE within the education sector who is not a medical expert or specialist to downplay your concerns pertaining to your child, their health, mental health, or education—and what they are entitled to under UK law.

You know your child when the school gates close. You see the exhaustion, the anxiety, the masking, and the tears that nobody else in a meeting room witnesses. Keep everything in writing, save your photos and daily logs, lean on the free charities and helplines inside this app, and take things one step at a time. You are doing an extraordinary job for your child.

How the EHCP Process Works Inside UK Schools

Mainstream schools use a 4-stage cycle called SEN Support (The Graduated Approach). Recording each cycle builds clear evidence of whether school adjustments are meeting your child's needs.

1

ASSESS

Class teacher and SENCO analyse needs using observations, assessments, and parent/child views.

2

PLAN

Agree adjustments, interventions, and review dates in a SEN Support plan, Pupil Passport, or Provision Map.

3

DO

Class/subject teachers carry day-to-day responsibility for teaching and adaptations, supported by TAs or specialists.

4

REVIEW

Review impact and progress with parents (typically termly) and adjust support or request an EHC Needs Assessment.

You Do Not Need School Permission or a Diagnosis to Apply

Under Section 36(1) of the Children and Families Act 2014, a parent has an independent legal right to request an EHC Needs Assessment directly from their Local Authority at any time. Schools must publish their SENCO contact details and SEN Information Report on their website.

                             UK Law & School Statutory Duties
                        
                        
Bullying Laws, Equality Act Duties & School Responsibilities

                        

                            Children with SEND are disproportionately targeted by bullying or unfair exclusion. Schools in England have strict statutory responsibilities.
                        

                    


                    

                        

                            
1. Equality Act 2010 — Disability & Reasonable Adjustments

                            

                                Schools must not discriminate against, harass, or victimise disabled pupils (including many neurodivergent, mental health, or chronic medical conditions). Under the anticipatory duty to make reasonable adjustments, schools must take reasonable steps so disabled pupils are not placed at a substantial disadvantage—and cannot charge parents for adjustments.
                            

                        

                        

                            
2. Education and Inspections Act 2006 (Section 89)

                            

                                Headteachers have a legal duty to determine measures in the school behaviour policy to encourage good behaviour and prevent all forms of bullying among pupils, communicate those measures to parents/pupils/staff, and address bullying including online or outside-school incidents affecting pupils.
                            

                        

                        

                            
3. Children & Families Act 2014 & Keeping Children Safe in Education

                            

                                Schools must use their "best endeavours" to secure special educational provision, safeguard pupils from child-on-child abuse and online harm under statutory safeguarding guidance (KCSIE), and where an EHCP is in place, the Local Authority must secure Section F provision (Section 42).
                            

                        

                        

                            
4. Complaints Escalation: Governors, Ofsted & EASS

                            

                                Keep a dated log of every bullying/safeguarding report. If a school fails to act, follow its formal Complaints Procedure (to Headteacher, then Governors/Academy Trust), escalate systemic safeguarding concerns to the Local Authority/DfE/Ofsted, and contact EASS, SENDIASS, or IPSEA.
